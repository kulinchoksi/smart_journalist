### **Priority Infrastructure Story 1: US Sanctions on Russia's Arctic LNG 2 Project**

**Full Project Details and Development Timelines:**
*   **Project Name:** Arctic LNG 2
*   **Location:** Gydan Peninsula, Siberia, Russia.
*   **Project Goal:** The project is a key element of Russia's ambition to increase its share of the global Liquefied Natural Gas (LNG) market to 20% by 2030, up from the current 8%.
*   **Development Status:** The project consists of three liquefaction trains (facilities). The first train, with a production capacity of 6.6 million metric tons per year, began production in December 2023 but has faced challenges in securing shipping tankers.
*   **Full Capacity:** The project is expected to have a total capacity of 19.8 million tons of LNG per year and 1.6 million tons per year of stable gas condensate upon completion of all three trains.
*   **Logistics:** The project requires a fleet of 15 Arc7 ice-class tankers, specifically designed for harsh Arctic conditions, which are being built at Russia's Zvezda shipyard.

**Infrastructure Investment and Financing Information:**
*   **Total Estimated Cost:** $21.3 billion.
*   **Project Operator & Majority Shareholder:** Novatek, Russia's largest LNG producer, holds a 60% stake.
*   **Other Shareholders:**
    *   TotalEnergies (France): 10%
    *   CNPC (China): 10%
    *   CNOOC (China): 10%
    *   Japan Arctic LNG (a consortium of Mitsui & Co. and JOGMEC): 10%
*   **Sanctions Impact on Financing:** The U.S. State Department imposed sanctions on the Limited Liability Company Arctic LNG 2, the project's operator. This prohibits U.S. nationals from doing business with the company and freezes its U.S. assets. The sanctions are expected to complicate the project's ability to secure financing for the remaining two trains. TotalEnergies has already stated it will not provide new financing for the project.

**Development Implications and Economic Impact:**
*   **Target:** The sanctions are part of a wider package against Russia over the war in Ukraine, aimed at curbing Moscow's future energy production and export capabilities.
*   **Immediate Impact:** The sanctions are likely to make it more difficult for Arctic LNG 2 to secure the specialized tankers it needs to ship its LNG, potentially stranding the gas produced. The project has already faced delays in tanker construction due to the withdrawal of foreign partners.
*   **Long-Term Impact:** The sanctions could lead to the withdrawal of other foreign partners and jeopardize the financing and completion of the project's second and third trains. This directly threatens Russia's strategic goal of capturing a 20% share of the global LNG market. The U.S. Treasury Department has allowed for a wind-down of transactions with Arctic LNG 2 until January 31, 2024, but the long-term outlook for exports and financing is now highly uncertain.
